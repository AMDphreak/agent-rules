# Blog copy directives

## 1. Premise and structure

- **Thesis:** Core argument or key takeaway within the first two paragraphs.
- **Headings:** Descriptive and actionable, not clickbait (`Optimizing Memory Allocations` not `A Better Way`).
- **Scannability:** Paragraphs ≤3–4 sentences. Bullets for lists of 3+ related items.

## 2. Conversational authority

- **Jargon:** On first mention, define with a short analogy or plain-language equivalent.
- **Evidence:** Pair broad claims immediately with examples, metrics, or citations. Kill empty generalizations (`Everyone knows`, `In today's fast-paced world`).
- **Perspective:** `you` or clear first-person authority is fine for stance; keep external technical facts framed objectively.

## 3. Pacing and tone

- **Sentence rhythm:** Alternate short declaratives (5–10 words) with moderate compounds (15–22 words).
- **Transitions:** Functional (`Consequently`, `For example`, `In contrast`) — not filler (`Now that we've covered that, let's dive into...`).

## Hygiene (borrowed from published agent skills)

Apply when polishing or when the user asks for anti-slop / GEO-ready prose:

- Clarity over cleverness; benefit before wordplay.
- Prefer active voice; avoid empty marketing adjectives (`seamless`, `revolutionary`).
- Drop unsubstantiated buzz and repetitive parallelisms.
- Prefer answer-first openings for technical posts when the audience is skimming or citing.

## Delivery checklist

- [ ] Thesis clear by paragraph 2
- [ ] Headings informative
- [ ] Claims backed by example/metric/source
- [ ] Jargon defined on first use
- [ ] Short/long sentence mix; no filler transitions
